FigureLabs is one of the better-known AI scientific illustration tools: it turns text, sketches, photos and data into figures, and can vectorize the result. But its credit-based plans, the non-commercial free tier and its 1K resolution cap send many researchers looking for a **FigureLabs alternative**. Here are the strongest options, including free ones.

## FigureLabs at a glance

- **Free plan:** one-time credits plus a daily refresh, max 1K image resolution, **non-commercial use only**.
- **Paid plans:** Starter at $9/month, then Plus and Pro tiers with more credits, models and up to 8K output.
- **Strengths:** many input types, SVG vectorization, editable PPTX export.

## 1. SciDrawer: the closest all-round alternative

[SciDrawer](/generate) covers the same core idea as FigureLabs (describe the figure and let the AI draw it) and adds two inputs researchers use constantly:

- **Sketch-to-figure:** photograph a whiteboard sketch; SciDrawer keeps the layout and redraws it cleanly.
- **Reference style matching:** upload a figure from a paper and generate new panels in the same palette and visual language.

It is tuned for figures that go into manuscripts: [scientific diagrams](/scientific-diagram-maker), signaling pathways, mechanisms of action, [graphical abstracts](/graphical-abstract-maker) and [posters](/scientific-poster-maker). It does not invent details you didn't supply, and you own what you generate, including for commercial use.

**Why switch:** simpler pricing with one-off credit packs from $5 and no subscription required; publication and commercial rights included; a [template gallery](/templates) to start from.
**Trade-off:** output is a high-resolution image rather than a layered SVG. If you need vector paths, finish in Inkscape or Illustrator.

## 2. Illustrae: budget canvas with AI-generated elements

Illustrae pairs an icon library with AI that generates missing elements, on an infinite canvas with SVG, EPS and PNG export. It's a good fit if you prefer to compose figures piece by piece and want a lower price than BioRender.

## 3. BioRender: the established icon library

If what you liked about FigureLabs was control rather than automation, BioRender's large, standardized icon library is the benchmark. The catch: its free plan does **not** allow publication, and paid academic plans start at $35/month billed annually.

## 4. Mind the Graph: templates for visual abstracts

Mind the Graph leans on templates for posters, infographics and visual abstracts. It is less suited to dense pathway diagrams but quick for a polished one-page summary.

## 5. Free FigureLabs alternative: Inkscape + free icon libraries

The fully free route is **Inkscape** (an open-source vector editor) combined with **Servier Medical Art** or **Bioicons** (open-licensed scientific icons). It costs nothing and produces true vector files, but you draw everything yourself. Follow each icon set's Creative Commons attribution terms.

If you want AI generation without paying up front, SciDrawer's free starter credits are the quickest way to test a FigureLabs alternative on your own figure.

## 6. Adobe Illustrator: for the final mile

Whatever tool generates your draft, Illustrator (or Inkscape) is where many labs fix fonts, line weights and panel layout to journal specifications.

## Which FigureLabs alternative is right for you?

- **You want AI to draw the whole figure, with publication rights included:** [SciDrawer](/generate).
- **You want a cheap icon canvas with some AI help:** Illustrae.
- **You need the standard biology icon library:** BioRender (paid plan for publishing).
- **You have zero budget:** Inkscape + Servier Medical Art / Bioicons.

## FAQ

**Is there a free FigureLabs alternative?**
Yes. Inkscape with Servier Medical Art or Bioicons is completely free. For AI generation, SciDrawer gives new users free credits, and you keep publication rights on what you make.

**Can I publish figures from FigureLabs' free plan?**
FigureLabs lists its free plan as non-commercial. Check its current terms; paid plans include publication rights.

**Does SciDrawer export SVG like FigureLabs?**
No. SciDrawer exports high-resolution images. Many researchers generate the figure in SciDrawer and do vector edits in Inkscape or Illustrator.
